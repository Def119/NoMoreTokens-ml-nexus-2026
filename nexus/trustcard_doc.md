Scope of This Document
This document explains the final selected model for the ML & AI Nexus 2026 Kaggle challenge: a frozen, calibrated ensemble evaluated under a 20-fold outer validation design. This summarizes validation strategy, predictive performance, calibration, robustness, subgroup reliability, uncertainty handling, explainability, model comparison, limitations, deployment recommendation, and reproducibility.
Alternative five-seed variant of the 20-fold design, and a related v6-derived candidate model was trained in parallel. Although this document does not talk about those models, certain comparisons and submissions to the kaggle competition were done via those models on getting to the final submission model.

1. Model Summary
The final selected model is a fixed, equal-weight ensemble of five component models: regularized logistic regression, cubic-spline logistic regression, LightGBM, CatBoost, and an Explainable Boosting Machine (EBM). The regularized logistic regression model utilizes clinically engineered features (such as comorbidity burden, abnormal vitals flags, and bed-day metrics), while the other four component models operate on raw electronic health record features to ensure structural model diversity. Each component model receives an equal weight of 0.20 in the ensemble. A sigmoid logistic calibrator is fit within each outer training partition using nested inner holdout predictions and applied to that partition's held-out predictions. Final test set predictions are generated by averaging the calibrated predictions across all 20 outer fold models. Patient identifiers are excluded from the feature set, and no external data or pretrained models are used.
The five component configurations and the calibration approach were frozen before this evaluation run. This keeps the selected model reproducible, while the team acknowledges that the decision to run a 20-fold campaign was made after inspecting results from earlier, smaller validation designs.

2. Validation Strategy
Validation uses 20 stratified outer folds with three inner folds nested inside each outer training partition, under a fixed random seed. Preprocessing steps (category vocabularies, numeric medians, feature representations, spline curves), tree stopping iterations (selected via median best rounds across inner folds for LightGBM and CatBoost), and sigmoid calibration parameters are learned strictly from the relevant training partition in each fold. Each outer fold is scored only on its own held-out rows, ensuring no row contributes to both training and evaluation within the same fold.
The training set contains 7,000 rows with 881 positive labels (12.59% prevalence). Out-of-fold predictions from all 20 outer folds are pooled to compute the performance metrics reported in this card.

3. Predictive Performance
On the pooled out-of-fold predictions, binary log loss is 0.3487788, Brier score is 0.1020847, ROC-AUC is 0.69366, and average precision is 0.28188. Calibration improves log loss only marginally over the uncalibrated ensemble (0.3488812 uncalibrated vs. 0.3487788 calibrated, an improvement of 0.0001024), indicating the raw ensemble output was already close to well-calibrated.
For operational context only, at a probability threshold of 0.15 the out-of-fold sensitivity is 0.511 and specificity is 0.765. Because the competition is scored on log loss rather than a fixed threshold, this operating point should not be used to judge the Kaggle submission itself.

The public Kaggle leaderboard score for this submission is 0.33507. This figure is external leaderboard context, self-reported by the team, and was not independently regenerated as part of this internal validation workflow.

4. Calibration
The out-of-fold calibration slope is 0.9864 and the intercept is -0.0252, both close to the ideal values of 1 and 0 respectively. Calibration parameters are fit from inner out-of-fold predictions inside each outer training partition, never from the outer holdout labels, which avoids leakage into the reported metrics.

Figure 1. Reliability curve and predicted-probability distribution for the raw and calibrated ensemble.

Calibration in this sense refers only to statistical agreement between predicted probabilities and observed event rates on this dataset; it is not a substitute for clinical validation.

5. Robustness and Stability
Across the 20 held-out folds, calibrated log loss has a mean of 0.3487788, a standard deviation of 0.0125862, a minimum of 0.3107431, and a maximum of 0.3813199, indicating moderate fold-to-fold variability.

Figure 2. Held-out binary log loss for each of the 20 outer folds.

Compared with a related fixed 10-fold calibrated design, the paired out-of-fold difference is -0.0001703, with a conditional paired-bootstrap 95% interval of [-0.0006262, +0.0002724]. Because this interval spans zero, the small local improvement over the 10-fold design is not a statistically confident claim of superiority. The interval also conditions only on saved out-of-fold predictions and does not account for training or tuning uncertainty, overlapping-fold dependence, or the effect of adaptive research choices made while developing the model.

6. Subgroup Reliability
Subgroup-level counts, prevalence, log loss, and Brier scores were computed across sex, rurality, hospital type, and age group.

Figure 4. Out-of-fold log loss by observed subgroup, with subgroup size and prevalence.

Performance is noticeably weaker for the oldest age group (75+), which also has the highest observed prevalence, and slightly weaker for rural patients relative to urban and semi-urban patients. These differences may reflect case mix and sampling variability across subgroups; they do not by themselves establish fairness, discrimination, or a causal relationship. Confidence intervals around subgroup estimates and prospective subgroup monitoring would be needed before drawing stronger conclusions.

7. Uncertainty and Human Oversight
Predictive uncertainty is estimated using entropy on the out-of-fold probabilities. Referring to the 10% most uncertain cases for additional review leaves 6,300 retained cases with a log loss of 0.3171099, a Brier score of 0.0888771, and an ROC-AUC of 0.63474. The retained set contains 90.0% of all rows, while the referred set has an observed prevalence of 35.0%, substantially higher than the overall base rate.
This referral analysis is a retrospective simulation of a human-review queue rather than evidence that referral improves outcomes in practice. Any entropy cutoff and its associated performance would need to be re-estimated prospectively before use in a live setting.

8. Explainability
Existing SHAP and sensitivity analyses were produced for a related, five-fold component recipe and are treated as contextual diagnostics rather than complete explanations of every prediction from the final 20-fold ensemble. Where used, these figures are labeled as being drawn from that related recipe rather than presented as final-model explanations.
This card intentionally emphasizes reproducible calibration, fold stability, threshold trade-offs, and subgroup log loss as its primary evidence, rather than presenting component-level feature explanations as if they fully explained the final ensemble. As with any explanation method, predictive association is not causation: correlated variables can share or mask importance, and a local explanation does not establish that changing a given feature would change the outcome.

9. Model Comparison
The 20-fold calibrated ensemble is selected as the final model because it is the strongest candidate with a confirmed public Kaggle score (0.33507). A related 20-fold, five-seed variant produced a lower validation log loss internally but was never submitted to Kaggle, so its leaderboard performance is unverified and it is not selected for this package. A later 30-fold, five-seed run was effectively tied with that unverified variant and is likewise not selected. An earlier model family (v6 and its derivatives) is excluded because it underperformed on the public leaderboard.

10. Failure Modes and Limitations
Performance may degrade if the deployment population differs from the synthetic training distribution used in this competition.
Changes in missingness patterns or care pathways over time are not represented in this validation and could affect reliability.
The model may be overconfident for patients whose feature combinations fall outside the training data's support.
Subgroup estimates for smaller groups (e.g., rural patients, the oldest age band) are based on limited sample sizes and may be unstable.
The public Kaggle score is a single leaderboard observation, not an external clinical validation.
No causal, fairness, or clinical-safety claim is made anywhere in this document.

11. Deployment / Use Recommendation
This model requires further development before any deployment beyond the competition setting. All results represent retrospective validation on a synthetic competition dataset. Before any patient-facing use, the model would require external validation on independent data, a prospective silent-mode evaluation period, ongoing calibration monitoring, formal subgroup review, drift detection, and appropriate clinical governance and sign-off.
Within the scope of this competition, the model documented here is recommended as the team's primary submission, with any less-tested alternative candidates evaluated separately before being considered for future use.

12. Reproducibility
The full evaluation run underlying this card is completely deterministic, with all randomness in fold partitioning, model parameter initialization, and inner cross-validation controlled by a single fixed master random seed: 20260914.
Preprocessing steps (category vocabularies, numeric medians, continuous feature representations) and probability calibration parameters are refit strictly within each training partition during execution, matching the original validation design and avoiding any leakage from held-out rows.
The competition test predictions are produced by evaluating and calibrating each of the 20 outer fold models on the test set and averaging the 20 resulting probabilities. The generated submission file was fully validated against the competition's required format (matching patient IDs, row count, column headers, and valid probability bounds between 0 and 1). Its SHA-256 cryptographic checksum is 6982221b0fb5e9d8098915f8352bcd2bc681d73dac0584ab325f19e346f05f11, confirming exact bitwise reproducibility from the model code.
